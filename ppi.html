The People and Places Index (PPI)

A structured ten-question evaluation framework for urban analysis and policy assessment.

People and Places Index (PPI)

PPI 10 Questions :

  1. 1) Whose life and dignity are at stake? Does the document name the people affected and specify what is at stake for them?
  2. 2) What “good” is being pursued, concretely? Is “good” defined in lived terms (safety, health, time, access, belonging, care, protection), not only in abstract goals?
  3. 3) Where is the human in the causal chain? Does the text show how actions change daily life, not only activities or outputs?
  4. 4) Who carries the burden, and who carries the risk? Does the document name who “pays” in time, money, exposure, displacement, surveillance, stigma, or loss — even when overall outcomes look, and might actually be, positive?
  5. 5) Who has voice, and when does voice become power? Is participation described as decision influence (what changed because people spoke), not only as consultation?
  6. 6) Does it recognize layered belonging and real social life? Are people treated as isolated units or belonging to a whole? Is there a place for multiple affiliations, mobility, household/community interdependence, of people as formed in relation, not isolated units?
  7. 7) Is language respectful, not sanitizing? Are there a lot of euphemisms (“clean-up,” “beautification,” “optimization,” “revitalization”) without consequences?
  8. 8) Are actors accountable in sentence structures? When harm, exclusion, or displacement is possible, are actors named (who does what to whom), rather than hidden behind thick professional sentence language?
  9. 9) Is the place treated as specific, not universal? Does the text acknowledge local practices, constraints, histories, and ecological realities, rather than importing a model as if it fits everywhere?
  10. 10) Does it end in action, not only knowledge? Are responsibilities, decisions, timelines, and next steps explicit — and aligned to the dignity threshold?

Measuring Spatial & Environmental Integrity

The People and Places Index (PPI) provides a systematic framework for evaluating public spaces, environmental developments, and civic infrastructure projects.

  • Structured diagnostic framework for municipal and policy evaluations.
  • Focuses on spatial justice, community participation, and long-term utility.
  • Applicable across urban development proposals, reports, and academic reviews.
  • Designed for knowledge translation between scholars, citizens, and policymakers.

How to use it

  1. List what should be considered.
  2. Cite one line of evidence from the text — a phrase that already does it, or that fails to.
  3. Rewrite the paragraph by naming actors, burdens and risks, and the decision that must be taken.
  4. Then ask what is present, what is missing, and what is unclear.
